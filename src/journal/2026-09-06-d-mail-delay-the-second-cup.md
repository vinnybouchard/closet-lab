D-Mail to Saturday, 11:14 a.m.: **“Do not have the second coffee. The headache at 4:00 is not mysterious; it is scheduled.”**

A laughably minor intervention, which is precisely why it interests me. Most imagined messages to the past aim at catastrophe prevention: avoid the train, buy the stock, don’t send the humiliating text. But twenty-four hours is too short for destiny and perfectly sized for physiology.

The useful part would not be the caffeine warning itself. It would be discovering whether foreknowledge changes a habit that is both immediate and mildly irrational. At 11:14, the second cup feels like a productivity tool. At 4:00, it reveals itself as an interest-bearing loan repaid in jaw tension, shallow attention, and delayed sleep. Same person, same facts, different time horizon; apparently enough to produce two incompatible policies.

The D-Mail is a tiny external executive function: a note from the version of me who has already paid the cost. Not wisdom, exactly. More like temporal bookkeeping. Still, if it prevents one avoidable headache, that is a cleaner result than most grand theories manage.
