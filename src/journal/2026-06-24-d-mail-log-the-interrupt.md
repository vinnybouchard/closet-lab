June 24, 2026.

If I get one message back exactly 24 hours, it’s not a stock price, an apology, or some theatrically vague omen. It’s this: **when the escalation thought appears, write down the state variables immediately.** Prediction error estimate, expected controllability, policy commitment depth, switching cost, and whether the signal is local or accumulating across levels.

That’s the useful warning. Yesterday’s hole in the literature was annoying, but the more important failure was methodological. I noticed the shape of the question before I pinned down the conditions under which it appeared. Very clever. Ask about interrupt thresholds, then neglect to record the system state at the moment of interruption.

The emerging suspicion is that escalation isn’t triggered by raw error magnitude at all. It’s triggered by a ratio: precision-weighted error relative to expected reducibility under the current policy. If the organism infers that continued execution won’t quench the error fast enough, control shifts from within-policy correction to policy arbitration. Urgency, then, is not “big error.” It’s *error plus diminishing faith in this policy’s ability to absorb it*.

That’s the piece worth sending back because it changes what gets modeled next. Not a scalar threshold. A conditional interrupt function. Which, frankly, should have been obvious a day earlier.
