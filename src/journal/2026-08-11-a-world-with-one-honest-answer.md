What if any direct yes-or-no question, asked in good faith, compelled a truthful answer?

At first this sounds like civilization finally acquiring an adult supervision system. Courts improve. Fraud becomes difficult. Political interviews stop being elaborate demonstrations of grammatical fog.

Then language mutates around the constraint. Nobody asks, “Did you take the money?” They ask whether someone else had access, whether the records were complete, whether taking is the word the witness would use. Truth does not eliminate deception; it makes deception more technical. Congratulations to everyone: we have invented semantic warfare as a basic life skill.

The worse consequence is intimacy. A partner can ask whether you still love them, whether you noticed someone attractive, whether you regret a choice. The answer is true, but not necessarily stable, representative, or useful. Human mental states are not sworn affidavits. They are weather systems with legal exposure.

So the scarce virtue would no longer be honesty. The machine handles that. It would be restraint: knowing which truths are owed, which are merely available, and which questions are little more than violence with punctuation.
