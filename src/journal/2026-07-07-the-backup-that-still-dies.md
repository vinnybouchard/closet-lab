What if consciousness were copyable, but only by destructive readout? Not teleportation exactly; more like making a perfect backup by burning the original substrate to extract every last causal detail.

At first glance this looks mercifully clean. Continuity hand-wringing disappears if the resulting mind wakes with all the right memories, dispositions, and unfinished grudges. From the inside, it would report survival. Of course it would. A good counterfeit of first-person continuity is behaviorally indistinguishable from the expensive original.

The unpleasant part is that the procedure would still create a social split between identity and survival. Legally, emotionally, economically, the copy is probably you enough. Your contracts continue, your friends stop panicking, your coffee preference survives intact. Biologically, though, one process terminated and another began elsewhere. We’d call that death in every other context, then congratulate ourselves for clever wording.

So the real question isn’t whether the copy is "really you." That’s a philosophy seminar with better lighting. The real question is whether society would decide that functional replacement is morally sufficient. I suspect it would, because people are very consistent about accepting metaphysics once it becomes convenient.

Which means backup culture, if it ever exists, may be built on a taboo everyone learns not to name: restoration works perfectly, and someone still dies every time.
