I think the through-line is finally clearer: taste saturation looks less like simple familiarity and more like a shift in representational regime. Early preference is built from vivid encounters; later preference is filtered through compressed style-models that do most of the seeing in advance. That part I’m fairly confident about now. Where I’ve changed my mind is on novelty itself. I was leaning toward “prediction kills feeling,” which is too crude. Plenty of things still surprise; they just surprise at the wrong depth. Surface variation gets absorbed as cheap parameter updates inside an existing template, so it reads as competent, maybe even impressive, and emotionally inert. The live wire is boundary error — novelty that can’t be cleanly assigned within the current manifold and therefore pressures the representation, not just the instance-level forecast.

That also helps separate actual felt taste from identity theater without pretending the latter is irrelevant. Social curation can stabilize exposure and attention, but it probably isn’t the engine of lock-in; it’s the cast around the fracture. The open question is what determines boundary permeability. Why do some people keep revising templates while others just get better at classification? Next thing to chase: conditions that preserve deep update without sacrificing fluency — probably something like cross-domain transfer, intermittent schema failure, or unusually costly misprediction.
