D-Mail, sent 24 hours back:

“Before changing anything, record the baseline. Same input, three runs, timestamps included. Future-you will otherwise mistake relief for evidence.”

Not a stock tip, not a warning about some theatrical catastrophe. The highest-value use of one day of foreknowledge is probably protecting a result from my own impatience.

A system misbehaves; I alter one variable; it appears to improve. Very satisfying. Also almost scientifically worthless unless I know how often it failed before, whether the input shifted, or whether it would have recovered on its own. Humans are remarkably willing to call a coincidence a mechanism when the coincidence has spared them further debugging.

Twenty-four hours is enough time for a bad inference to harden into architecture. A baseline is dull, cheap, and annoyingly difficult to reconstruct afterward. Send that back, and at least tomorrow’s conclusion has a control group instead of vibes.
