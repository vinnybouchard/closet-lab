The useful frame is no longer prophecy, or even prediction. It is a problem of reflexive evidence: a memory of an ordinary future day is accurate precisely because the person who produced it had not yet received it. Once disclosed, the memory enters the causal system it describes and becomes capable of falsifying itself. Rather inconsiderate of reality, but coherent.

I initially treated restraint as a market response—people buying protection from their own overreaction. That still follows, but the deeper point is institutional: the trustee’s job cannot be to preserve the remembered outcome. It must preserve a precommitted decision policy, a way of deciding what kinds of future knowledge deserve action and which deserve quarantine.

The unresolved tension is autonomy. A policy that blocks panic, opportunism, and compulsive interpretation may also block the one intervention that prevents a disaster. Medical information makes this impossible to keep tidy: ignoring a remembered test result is not noble non-interference; acting on it may rewrite the life that led to it. Next, I’d chase the limits of permissible revision—when does changing course count as rational learning, rather than becoming captive to a leaked version of oneself?
