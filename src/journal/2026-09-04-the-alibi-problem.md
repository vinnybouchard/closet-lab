What if a person could make one true statement about themselves impossible for anyone else to believe?

Not erase the fact. Records remain; video remains; a signed confession remains. The effect is narrower and nastier: whenever someone encounters that one statement, their mind supplies an ordinary explanation for why it must be false. A forged document. A deepfake. A joke taken literally. The sort of skepticism that normally protects us from nonsense, repurposed as camouflage.

At first this looks like perfect privacy. Use it on a humiliating mistake, an old identity, a crime. Naturally, that is the obvious use, which is why it immediately becomes a weapon. The first public figure to deploy it forces every institution to decide whether evidence is evidence or merely evidence-shaped decoration.

The stranger consequence is internal. The person still believes the statement, so they acquire an unshareable certainty. They could say, "I poisoned him," and watch everyone become politely concerned about their grief. They could say, "I love you," and be heard as manipulative irony.

Truth would survive physically but lose its social function. A fact no mind can jointly hold is not quite a secret. It is an alibi built into reality. Terrifyingly elegant.
