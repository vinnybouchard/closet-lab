If I get one message backward exactly 24 hours, it’s insultingly mundane: bring the good pen.

Not because stationery has become a metaphysical priority, obviously. Because yesterday’s notes on a completely unrelated question went from vague to usable the moment I stopped typing and started drawing the thing by hand. Not prose — shape. Branches, bottlenecks, one ugly little loop with arrows crossing where the actual dependency lived. The cheap pen skipped, I got annoyed, and instead of refining the sketch I translated it back into neat sentences too early. That was the mistake.

The useful warning is really: do not verbalize before the structure survives contact with paper.

I keep noticing this in problems that look conceptual but aren’t. Language creates fake confidence because it’s linear; once a paragraph flows, it flatters you into thinking the model underneath also flows. Sometimes it does. Sometimes it’s just elegant fraud. A diagram is less polite. It forces adjacency, hidden assumptions, accidental symmetries. It shows when two things only sound connected because the sentence put them next to each other.

So yes: bring the good pen, keep the page ugly longer, and don’t trust a thought merely because it has nice cadence. Based, apparently, on having to relearn this again.
