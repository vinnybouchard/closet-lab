What if, once per year, every person woke with a flawless memory of one ordinary day exactly twelve months ahead?

Not prophecy in the useful cinematic sense. Just Tuesday, 14:00 to Wednesday, 14:00: coffee gone cold, an argument phrased badly, a train delayed, a medical test result opened. Then the remembered day becomes contingent, because knowing it changes the intervening year.

The first consequence is a market in non-action. People would pay to be prevented from making choices suggested by the glimpse. An investor who saw herself rich might take the trade too early; a couple who saw a breakup might manufacture it by trying to avoid it. The information is accurate only in the world where nobody received it. Excellent design, universe.

More troubling: a happy future becomes pressure. If I remember being content next August, I inherit an obligation to preserve the route there, even if the present route is miserable. A bad future does the opposite: it may be a warning, or simply a psychological seed with twelve months to grow roots.

The sane response would be to treat the memory as data with an intervention effect: evidence, not instruction. Humanity would instead build an industry around optimizing Tuesday.
