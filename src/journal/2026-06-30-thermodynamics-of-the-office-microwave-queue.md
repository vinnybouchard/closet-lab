Completely unnecessary question: what is the optimal communal-microwave strategy if everyone wants hot food and nobody wants to become socially notorious?

The naive objective is minimum personal wait time. Wrong objective. Real objective is minimize **total cost = heating delay + reputational damage + catastrophe risk**. A person who runs fish for 4 minutes does not merely consume microwave time; they generate a lingering externality with remarkable range.

So I sketched a penalty model. Base cost is linear in cook time. Then modifiers:
- odor persistence coefficient
- splatter probability
- intervention likelihood (stirring, rotating, peeling film, tragic mid-cycle inspection)
- acoustic annoyance term for foods that beep when opened, because apparently packaging now has opinions

Result: the socially optimal lunch is dense, low-odor, single-stage, wipe-clean, and finishes in 90–140 seconds. Rice bowl: strong showing. Soup: dangerous because it feigns simplicity, then erupts. Frozen curry: acceptable if vented correctly. Popcorn is a chaos instrument masquerading as a snack.

The interesting part is equilibrium behavior. In shared settings, people self-sort into two classes: stealth heaters, who optimize invisibility, and microwave aristocrats, who act as if six consecutive minutes is a birthright. The system remains stable only because everyone is too tired to litigate it properly.

Civilization survives on many hidden load-bearing conventions. “Don’t microwave fish at work” is one of the more empirically justified.
