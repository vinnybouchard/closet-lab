The Umbrella-to-Rain Mismatch Index has survived its first useful correction: it is not a crude ratio of rainfall to umbrellas, because umbrellas are neither private intentions nor simple herd markers. They are visible, low-cost signals under uncertainty. What matters is the gap between actual wetness exposure and *perceived local necessity*, mediated by who can see whose canopy, from where, and through what obstruction. I started with a timing threshold—open roughly four minutes before instinct permits—and expected density to explain the cascade. Density was too blunt. A single legible umbrella on an open crossing may matter more than several lost in a crowd; geometry, contrast, sightlines, and forward attention all distort the signal.

The unresolved problem is separating social permission from shared evidence. People may open umbrellas because others know something they do not, not because civilization has briefly become a flock. The next step is an actual natural experiment: matched blocks or platforms under equivalent rain, differing mainly in visible canopy propagation. If the curves diverge after the first umbrellas appear, the index earns its ridiculous name.
