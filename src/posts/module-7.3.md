  angle: >-
    "The risk register for a server in a closet." The final post, and the one that does
    the most work for a career: it demonstrates the thing employers actually cannot
    teach — **that you can look at a system you built, name what could go wrong, and
    make a defensible decision about each one.**
  evidence_to_capture: >-
    The register itself, with the accepted risks and their triggers left in. Do not
    clean it up. A register with six mitigations and six documented acceptances is more
    credible than one that claims everything is fixed.
  the_honest_part: >-
    Six of twelve rows said *decide* when you started, and the register did not exist
    until you wrote it. That is true of nearly every system anybody runs, and being the
    person who wrote it down is the entire point.
  what_to_redact: >-
    Do not publish a live risk register naming unremediated findings on a reachable
    machine — that is a target list. Publish the **method and the shape**: the
    categories, the four treatments, the revisit-trigger discipline, and one or two
    resolved examples. The unfixed rows stay in `courses/`, which is private, which is
    the boundary this course has been about from module 5.4 onward. ---
